Creative Writer · Obsidian plugin · ux review and redesign

Everything that touches the page lives in the editor. Everything about the work lives in panels.

The plugin already holds that line. What it does not yet hold is one vocabulary: five panels invent five layouts, five colour systems and five ways to reach an action, and the editor spends its one scarce channel — the underline — on three unrelated things at once. This review goes screen by screen, ranks the ten changes worth making first, and redesigns three panels around a shared shell.

01

Heuristic evaluation, screen by screen

hierarchy · density · typography · colour semantics · empty states · discoverability · consistency
01

Editor: rhythm, style checks, focus fade

  • colour semantics
    Rhythm underlines, style-check tints, spell-check squiggles and internal links all land on the same channel under the same words. A measurement (sentence length) is rendered in the visual language of a fault.
  • density
    Four layers are live on the cursor paragraph at once — fade, band, underline, tint. There is no one-key way to see the prose plain.
  • hierarchy
    The current-line band runs the full viewport width, well past the text column, so the widest object on screen is a cursor affordance.
  • discoverability
    Nothing on the page says what warm versus cool means. The legend exists only in the settings tab.
02

Zen mode

  • works
    Chrome is gone and the measure is preserved. This is the strongest screen in the set.
  • density
    Every decoration layer survives into zen. The one place ink should be quietest is as loud as the normal editor.
  • hierarchy
    The line band bleeds to the window edge, reading as leftover UI in a mode defined by having none.
  • discoverability
    No exit affordance and no word count. A writer who entered zen by command palette has to guess at Esc.
03

Writing desk sidebar

  • hierarchy
    "0 words" is the largest thing in the panel and the least useful: it is a raw count, not a verdict. What a novelist wants at a glance is how far behind they are.
  • density
    The heatmap key is a two-sentence paragraph doing a tooltip's job, and it is longer than the heatmap.
  • typography
    Counts are set in the UI font without tabular figures, so "232 / 3,000 · 8%" and "844 / 20,000 · 4%" do not align down the column.
  • empty states
    Three voices in four lines: "Nothing added this week.", a bare projected date, and "Open a note to see how it reads." None says what to do next.
  • colour semantics
    Purple heatmap squares mean "mostly revision" and also happen to be the accent colour used for interactive elements everywhere else.
04

Writer board

  • colour semantics
    Six group hues with tinted backgrounds, none of them the theme accent, and loose chips outlined in red — which reads as an error state rather than "unfiled".
  • hierarchy
    Two label layers say the same word: the section label "STORIES" sits directly above the group header "Stories · 5".
  • density
    Cards carry four analysis metrics (ease, grade, variety, dialogue) plus an identical italic placeholder about missing premises. The card stops being an identity and becomes a report.
  • discoverability
    Every action lives behind one unlabelled icon, and three of them — "Fit", "Shake", "Copy schema" — cannot be guessed from their labels.
  • consistency
    Empty groups explain themselves ("Places and milieus you return to."); filled groups never do. Cards clip at the right edge with no scroll affordance.
05

Story map

  • empty states
    "Nothing matches the current filters." is the worst state in the plugin: it names no filter, offers no button, and sits opposite a panel where "Hide loners" is on and the project has one node. The user is told they are wrong without being told how.
  • density
    Nine toggles and eight colour rows are expanded by default, and toggle switches — a settings control — are used for what is really per-kind visibility.
  • hierarchy
    Five buttons at equal weight. "Read project with model" is slow and consequential; it looks exactly like "Fit".
  • colour semantics
    Kind swatches are saturated web colours that ignore the theme, and only one row ("Notes · 1") carries a count, so the panel cannot tell you what the project actually contains.
  • discoverability
    The panel floats over the right third of the canvas and covers the nodes it is filtering.
06

Story timeline

  • hierarchy
    The matrix occupies the left quarter of a full-width tab; four fifths of the screen is empty. Column width should grow with the cast.
  • typography
    Rotated cast names at roughly 10px are the hardest text to read in the plugin, and they are the axis you scan constantly.
  • colour semantics
    Blue, green and grey dots, plus blue and green names, with no key anywhere. On stage, mentioned and absent are indistinguishable to a new user.
  • density
    Rows carry word counts only. Chapter rows show no totals, so the one structural question the view could answer — is this chapter light? — goes unanswered.
  • consistency
    This is the only panel with its controls in a top bar rather than the floating panel, and the only one with no jump buttons to its siblings.
07

Story threads

  • hierarchy
    Strips are labelled below themselves in small grey text, and the label slot carries an axis note ("· max 2") instead of a unit. Reading the chart requires reading upward.
  • colour semantics
    Five strips of identical purple for five different quantities, and orange versus blue arcs with no key. Colour is decoration here, not information.
  • empty states
    "No facts read yet." sits under the CONTRADICTIONS heading while a real, actionable warning — a letter pointing at no scene — is stranded in the THREADS section above it. The urgent item has the quietest home.
  • discoverability
    Nothing in the view suggests what to do about a contradiction: no click target, no jump to the scene.
08

Manuscript and comments pane

  • hierarchy
    Two lists compete in one column: "This paragraph" and "All comments (11)". The selected paragraph gets a purple outline heavier than the prose inside it.
  • typography
    Two vocabularies for one concept: six uppercase kinds (TODO, FIX, CHECK, IDEA, CUT, REF) and two lowercase ones (note, mark). Users will read them as different systems, because they look like different systems.
  • colour semantics
    Chip colours run yellow, green, red, grey with no stated meaning, and "mark" (a highlight) and "FIX" (an action) sit at the same weight.
  • density
    The chapter name — the one piece of context needed to navigate the list — is right-aligned in the faintest colour available.
  • discoverability
    The composer placeholder is a syntax lesson, and "Enter appends it to the end of this paragraph" describes a side effect on the manuscript that the composer's position does not imply.
02

Top ten changes, ranked by impact for a daily novelist

  1. 01
    Every empty state names its cause and carries the fix
    "Hide loners is hiding 1 node" with a button beside it, not "Nothing matches the current filters." Applies to the map, threads, readability and empty board groups. Cheapest change with the largest effect on whether the panels feel broken.
    all panels · copy + 1 button
  2. 02
    One colour dictionary, built from theme variables
    Three meanings only: accent for what the writer made, warning for what needs checking, muted for what the model derived. Retire the six board hues and the saturated map swatches; derive kind colours from the accent's hue ramp so community themes stay coherent.
    system · medium
  3. 03
    Take sentence rhythm off the underline channel
    Underlines belong to spelling and links. Rhythm moves to a gutter meter in the left margin — one tick per sentence, length by width — and ships off by default. Style checks keep the tint, at half the current alpha, and only inside the cursor paragraph.
    editor · medium
  4. 04
    One panel shell for all five panels
    Docked right, not floating over the canvas. Same header: scope dropdown, search, overflow. Same body: native setting rows, everything collapsed except the section you last used. Same footer: sibling jumps. Learning one panel then teaches the other four.
    all panels · large
  5. 05
    Comments become one grouped list with the current paragraph pinned
    Sticky chapter headers replace the faint right-aligned labels; the selected paragraph's comments pin to the top with a two-line quote. Revision means moving between comments all day, so this is the highest-traffic list in the plugin.
    manuscript · medium
  6. 06
    The desk leads with a verdict, not a count
    "120 to go" over "380 of 500", with pace as a single short clause. Counts set in tabular monospace so project rows align. The heatmap key becomes a four-square legend plus a tooltip.
    desk · small
  7. 07
    Visibility stops being a toggle switch
    Kind and filter rows get a clickable-icon eye plus a count, and kinds absent from the project collapse into one faint line. The map panel loses roughly half its height without losing a control.
    map, threads · small
  8. 08
    One filled button per panel, and it is the expensive one
    "Read project with model" is the only accent button, with its cost stated beneath it. Fit, shake and copy schema move into the overflow menu, where each row shows its command name so the writer can bind their own key.
    all panels · small
  9. 09
    Timeline and threads get a key and the whole pane
    A one-line legend under the header defines every dot and arc. The matrix stretches to the tab width, cast names go horizontal on a sticky header row, and chapter rows carry totals. Strip labels move above their strips with a unit.
    timeline, threads · medium
  10. 10
    Zen mode drops to one layer and admits one indicator
    Focus fade only; no band, no rhythm, no tints. A word count and an exit hint fade in on mouse move and out after two seconds, both inside the text column.
    editor · small

The shared panel shell

the contract the three artboards below all obey
scope
First row, always: which story this panel is about, then search. Never below an action.
state line
One monospace line under scope: what is in the project, what is hidden, and a reset. The panel's summary of itself.
sections
Native setting rows in collapsed groups, each header showing its current value so it can be read without opening.
footer
Sibling jumps in the same order in all five panels: desk, board, map, timeline, threads. Current panel shown, not linked.

desk board map timeline threads
  1. 01
    Docked, not floating
    The panel is a sibling of the canvas rather than a sheet on top of it, so filtering never hides the thing being filtered. It also inherits Obsidian's own resize handle for free.
  2. 02
    The state line carries the truth
    "14 nodes · 0 shown · 2 filters on" plus reset. The user learns the graph is not empty before they learn anything else, and reset is one click from the diagnosis.
  3. 03
    The empty state explains and repairs
    It names both filters responsible and offers exactly the two buttons that undo them. The command palette hint teaches the keyboard route without claiming a hotkey.
  4. 04
    One accent button, with its price on the label's line
    Cost and last-run time sit beneath "Read project with model". Fit, shake and copy schema live in the ⋯ menu, each row naming its command so the writer can bind a key themselves.
  5. 05
    Kinds show counts and hide absences
    Only kinds present in the project get a row; the rest collapse to one faint line. Swatches are the accent hue ramp rather than fixed web colours, so a community theme restyles them. An eye icon replaces the switch: it reads as visibility, is half the height, and matches the core graph view.
  6. 06
    Collapsed sections state their value
    Filters says "2 on" in warning colour, display says "labels, arrows". Three former panels of switches are now three readable rows, and the active filter count is the only warning-coloured thing in the panel.

  1. 01
    One list, one scroll
    The "this paragraph" block is pinned above the same list rather than being a second list. It holds the quote, the location, and the composer's target — so the writer always knows where a new comment will land.
  2. 02
    Section headers replace the faint right column
    Chapter names become sticky headers with a count and an open count. Navigation context moves from the weakest colour in the panel to the strongest.
  3. 03
    Kinds get one casing and three colours
    All lowercase monospace, as tags in the note actually are. Accent for what you must do (todo, fix), warning for what you must verify (check), muted for thinking and marks (idea, note, mark, ref), and cut struck through at reduced opacity. Colour lives in a 2px bar, not a filled chip, so the comment text stays the loudest thing in the row.
  4. 04
    Anchor text under the comment
    One clamped italic line of the prose the comment is attached to. It is what makes an eleven-item list navigable without clicking each row.
  5. 05
    Resolve is per row, on hover
    A clickable-icon tick appears on the hovered row (shown on the first item). Resolved comments leave the open count but stay in the list, dimmed, because writers revisit their own reasoning.
  6. 06
    The composer states its side effect once
    Kind moves out of the placeholder into a dropdown; the placeholder says what the field is for; the mono line underneath says where the text goes and which keys commit it. No syntax lesson in a placeholder.

desk board map timeline threads
  1. 01
    The headline is the decision, not the measurement
    "120 to go" answers the question a novelist opens this panel with. The raw count drops to a mono subline, and pace is a two-word verdict in the corner rather than a sentence.
  2. 02
    Streak facts collapse to one mono row
    Streak, best and week sit on a single line in tabular figures instead of a three-item header above the heatmap.
  3. 03
    The heatmap key is a key
    Four swatches for volume, a ring for goal met, and detail on hover. Two sentences of prose become nine characters. The section header carries the only number worth stating up front: days written out of days elapsed.
  4. 04
    Revision is intensity, not a second hue
    One accent ramp for words touched; goal met becomes an outline. That frees green and amber to mean only pace, everywhere in the plugin.
  5. 05
    Project rows read as a column
    Name, tabular count, bar, then one verdict clause with a dot: stalled in amber, on pace in green. The projected date moves to a labelled mono line, so "7 oct" is never mistaken for a deadline the plugin knows about.
  6. 06
    Readability names its subject and says one useful thing
    Four numbers in a row with the note's name beside the header, plus a plain-language line. With no note open the section header stays and the body reads "open a note and this fills in" — the panel never loses a row.
